The Works of Rizal

A doctor by training and a writer by calling, Rizal turned his observations into stories that colonial authorities could not ignore. Browse his major novels, poems, and essays, with summaries of the plots, the historical background, and the lessons each one carries.

Noli Me Tángere

Rizal Exposes the Friars in a Novel

Rizal’s first novel, published in 1887. It follows Crisóstomo Ibarra, who returns from Europe and runs into the abuses of the Spanish friars and colonial officials.

The Sequel Where Things Get Darker

The 1891 sequel. Ibarra returns in disguise as the jeweler Simoun, and the story turns from reform to revenge.

Mi Último Adiós

The Farewell Poem Rizal Left Behind

The poem Rizal wrote the night before his execution on December 30, 1896. It’s his farewell to the country he loved.

Writing Timeline

  • 1887 Noli Me Tángere, printed in Berlin
  • 1891 El Filibusterismo, printed in Ghent
  • 1896 Mi Último Adiós, written before his execution

Key Characters

  • Crisóstomo Ibarra: the idealist who returns from Europe
  • María Clara: Ibarra's beloved
  • Elías: the fugitive who helps Ibarra
  • Simoun: Ibarra in disguise in the sequel
  • Basilio: the student whose story ties both novels together

Did You Know?

“Noli me tangere” is Latin for “touch me not.” Rizal took the phrase from the Gospel of John, and used it to describe a society's wounds that no one dared to touch.
